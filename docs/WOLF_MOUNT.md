# Wolf tiny mount

Wolf extends the existing tiny-mount registry, saddle slot, direct steering and relative-size policy. The bundled definition permits a maximum rider/mount effective SCALE ratio of 0.76. Wolves must be adult. Any player can saddle a tamed wolf, regardless of ownership or rider size; wild wolves reject saddles with a specific message. Wolf Armor uses BODY and the saddle uses SADDLE, and both remain on dismount.

For a **tamed wolf**, ordinary Use (right-click by default) with an empty hand mounts it when saddled; without a saddle, vanilla owner sit/stand behaviour remains. Holding a spare saddle also permits mounting an already-saddled wolf. Food, dye, repair and other item-specific interactions retain their normal meaning. Crouch + Use opens its equipment inventory, including when unsaddled. An oversized rider cannot mount or toggle a saddled wolf. Riding checks relative size, available seat and the vanilla boarding cooldown; borrowing never changes ownership. Crouch dismounts normally. Removing a saddle during an existing ride retains a passive passenger without control.

Wild wolves and other configured tameables keep Crouch + Use as their ride gesture, with held items preserved. Release Crouch before pressing it again to dismount after such a gesture. `item_steered` mounts require a saddle even to mount. Vanilla horse/pig/strider controllers are not replaced.

Tiny Mount families retain their equipment contracts. `direct` mounts such as Chicken accept passive unsaddled passengers and expose equipment through Crouch + Use or E while riding. `tameable_direct` equipment requires taming; control requires a saddle. Tamed wolves use the interaction exception described above; other configured tameables retain Crouch + Use mounting. Wolf declares native BODY Wolf Armor in its inventory. `item_steered` mounts such as Bee have no mount inventory: E opens the normal player inventory, as on Pig/Strider. Menus expose native SADDLE/BODY slots, not duplicate storage. Wolf BODY equipment remains owner-protected when borrowed.

Vanilla equipment components remain authoritative for shearing. The vanilla saddle, horse armor and wolf armor are shearable in 26.2; Tiny Mounts does not add a separate JSON switch. Wolf's native ownership rule therefore means only its owner can shear its equipment. Vanilla `EquipmentSlot` order removes BODY before SADDLE when both are present. Saddles can also be equipped through the vanilla dispenser equipment path for configured Tiny Mounts, including species that vanilla would not normally allow to wear one.

Wild adult wolves allow uncontrolled taming attempts. Each sustained ride lasts 60 server ticks (three seconds), then rolls `random.nextInt(100) < trust`, following the horse temper pattern. Trust starts at zero, rises by 5 after a failed complete ride and is capped at 100. Failure ejects the player; success tames the wolf to the current rider and clears the trust. An unfed first ride fails, while maximum trust guarantees the next complete ride; there is no fixed three-attempt rule.

Normal accepted bones retain vanilla's independent 1-in-3 direct taming chance. An unsuccessful accepted bone adds 10 trust for future riding attempts; rejected interactions, including angry wolves and Crouch + Use mounting while holding bones, add nothing and consume nothing. The trust value is stored with the wolf under `scalebrews:trust`, persists across saving/unloading/restarts, and is shared across riders like horse temper. Older saves default to zero and loaded values are clamped to 0–100. Only the current ride timer is transient: dismount or a rider change resets it, and partial rides add no trust.

Dismounting a still-wild wolf, voluntarily or by ejection, makes it target that rider. Normal survival combat rules still apply. Once tamed, dismounting does not provoke aggression. Saddle-free riding still does not grant control after taming.

WASD controls the wolf using the rider's look direction. Its configured controlled speed is 0.20, deliberately below a typical good horse so the wolf remains a combat/technical-terrain mount rather than replacing sustained horse travel. The server owns movement and attacks. A short Space press (up to three server ticks) attempts the wolf's normal forward attack; holding Space charges a pounce over ten ticks. After the tap-attack threshold, charging progressively reduces ground speed and reaches 50% of normal controlled speed at full charge. The existing horse jump HUD shows charge. Release launches along yaw with the established bounded pitch-dependent trajectory; gamefeel changes do not increase the pounce's existing travel envelope.

The local rider receives anticipation and impact feedback without changing server pounce physics: the wolf squashes progressively while charging, stretches along its travel axis during the pounce, the camera receives a short FOV kick, and hits/strong landings produce a brief damped camera impulse. Landing also emits a gravity-aware ring of the supporting block's vanilla dust particles plus a small fall sound. The effects are intentionally short rather than continuous so the pounce reads as forceful without turning travel into permanent camera shake.

When an optional gravity provider supplies a non-DOWN frame for the root wolf, the newly generated pounce impulse, target-height sampling, landing cleanup and landing dust use that frame's local forward/up axes; already-existing world momentum is not retroactively rotated. The first valid target intersected during a pounce receives one normal wolf attack. The action cooldown uses the vanilla melee goal's 20-tick interval. Landing removes residual pounce sliding in local-horizontal axes.

Commanded bites emit the native main-hand swing animation, including a missed tap and a pounce hit. The wolf advances the native swing clock on both sides and briefly enters the synced melee combat pose; this does not assign an AI target or create anger. Only a pose introduced by the command is cleared when the swing finishes, including after dismount. Resource packs can consume the native attack progress (`swing_progress` in EMF). The vanilla wolf model has no dedicated bite animation. Installed Fresh Animations 1.10.5 uses `is_aggressive` for combat poses but does not define a bite driven by attack progress; this integration activates those existing poses without inventing a jaw animation or requiring a resource pack.

Tiny-mount saddles and mounted riders consume a single `SeatFrame` resolved from the final complete model hierarchy after normal `setupAnim` and optional EMF/Fresh Animations work. The unit saddle follows the full affine frame; the rider follows its translation and orthonormal rotation without inheriting model scale. Submission is same-frame and limited to configured Tiny Mount pairs, so native horse rendering is unchanged.

The final render boundary was also exercised in a separate pinned runtime proof using the exact VanillaPlus stack: Entity Model Features 3.3.5, Entity Texture Features 7.2 and Fresh Animations 1.10.5. The proof required a real Fresh Animations Bee model backed by `EMFModelPartRoot`, a genuinely mounted player, and a finite non-identity rider transform produced by Scale Brews after EMF animation. The same client run produced visual snapshots with Fresh Animations active, including the mounted Bee and the wolf's saddle/armor. This diagnostic ran on `diagnostic/mount-emf-runtime` at `e0231522` (GitHub Actions run 34819886434); the optional compatibility stack is intentionally not a dependency of ordinary CI.

The saddle uses the existing hand-authored leather pixel art on shared unit geometry sized from the detected upper face. An optional resource-pack visual profile can refine the Wolf path or dimensions and coexists with the vanilla armor layer. No image-generation assets are used.

Development validation covers shared mounting, owner and equipment preservation, effective-scale ratios, real damage attribution, transient commanded-attack scope, six cardinal gravity frames, frame changes between actions, preservation of pre-existing world momentum, the controlled-speed/charge-slowdown contract, family-driven tiny-mount equipment and inventory behavior, native shearing priority, real dispenser saddle equipment, and client acceptance for equipment/player inventory screens and animated riders. Gravity support is a property of the root mount's effective frame, not a special Clinging/Tiny-Mount protocol. Additional two-player manual acceptance remains necessary for latency, team/PvP combinations, the complete betrayal interaction and subjective gamefeel tuning.
